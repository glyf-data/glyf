## What you may do without asking

You may use the word "glyf" in plain text to refer to this project truthfully.
For example:

- "We build our dashboards with glyf."
- "This plugin adds a Snowflake adapter for glyf."
- "Based on glyf" or "Derived from glyf" on a fork, with a link back to
  <https://github.com/glyf-data/glyf>.
- Naming a compatible or complementary project in a way that makes the
  relationship clear and does not suggest the Owner endorses it, such as
  "acme-glyf-plugin" or "glyf adapter for Acme".
- Unmodified copies of the official packages (`glyf-core` on PyPI, the
  `glyf-core` crate) redistributed as is, with the LICENSE and NOTICE files
  intact.
- Blog posts, talks, tutorials, and reviews about glyf, including the logo
  where it identifies this project.

## Forks and derived work

Forking is welcome and is exactly what the Apache License is for. The rules
above amount to one thing: a fork carries its own name, keeps the NOTICE and
LICENSE files, and says where it came from. "Built on glyf" is encouraged.
Calling the fork "glyf" is not.

If you see a use of the Marks that appears to violate this policy, or you are
unsure whether a planned use is permitted, open an issue at
<https://github.com/glyf-data/glyf/issues> or contact the Owner through the
channels listed in [SUPPORT.md](./SUPPORT.md). Permission requests are
usually answered quickly and are often granted for community projects.
